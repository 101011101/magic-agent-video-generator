# Research: Copywriting and Short-Form Video Story Structures

> Research input for [02-creative-director.md](../subsystems/02-creative-director.md). Collected 2026-10-04 by a research agent.
> Labels: **unverified** = no checked source supports it. **illustrative** = example written by the agent.

## Foundations

**Eugene Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966)**
- Mass desire: copy cannot create desire. It focuses desires that already exist onto a product.
- The awareness level decides the opening:

| Awareness level | What the opening does |
|---|---|
| Most aware | States the product and the price or offer. |
| Product aware | Reinforces desire, shows life after purchase, proves superiority, explains the mechanism, removes doubt. |
| Solution aware | Names the desire and/or its solution. |
| Problem aware | Names the need and dramatizes it; the product is the inevitable answer. |
| Completely unaware | Echoes an emotion or attitude. No product, no price. |

- Market sophistication decides the claim:

| Stage | Claim | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. First in market | Simple direct claim | "Now! Lose Ugly Fat!" |
| 2. Second in market | Bigger claim | "Lose up to 47 pounds in 4 weeks…" |
| 3. Claims stop working | New mechanism | "Floats Wonder Drug For Reducing" |
| 4. Mechanism copied | Bigger mechanism | "First No-Diet Wonder Drug…" |
| 5. Disbelief | Identification with the prospect | — |

**Joseph Sugarman, *Adweek Copywriting Handbook***
- Slippery slide: each line exists to get the next line read.
- Seeds of curiosity; sell a concept, not a product; sell on emotion, justify with logic; raise the objection yourself.
- 24 psychological triggers, including specificity, proof of value, credibility, urgency, curiosity, belonging, hope.

**Direct-response principles**
- One big idea (Ogilvy).
- Rule of One (Masterson & Forde): one idea, one emotion, one promise, one story, one desired response.
- Specificity, proof, a specific CTA, objection handling.

## Classic formulas

No source gives 15/30/60 s proportions for the classic formulas.

| Formula | Stages in order | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| AIDA (Lewis, 1898) | Attention → Interest → Desire → Action | General default |
| PAS (Kennedy) | Problem → Agitate → Solution | Problem-aware viewers |
| BAB (origin unverified) | Before → After → Bridge | Transformation products |
| 4Ps (Hoke/Mayer, secondary) | Promise → Picture → Proof → Push | Viewers who need belief |
| PASTOR (Ray Edwards) | Problem → Amplify → Story → Transformation/Testimony → Offer → Response | Longer copy, 60 s+ |
| FAB (origin unverified) | Feature → Advantage → Benefit | Product demos |
| 4Us (Masterson / AWAI) | Test for a hook: Useful, Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific | Scoring the first line |
| Hook–Story–Offer (Brunson) | Hook → Story → Offer | Creator and founder ads |
| StoryBrand SB7 (Miller) | Character (customer) wants → Problem → Guide (brand) → Plan → CTA → Avoid failure → Success | Brand messaging, explainers |

## Short-form video (platform sources)

**TikTok Creative Codes**
- Hook → Body → Close.
- First 6 s: 90% of ad recall impact, 80% of awareness impact.
- Product on screen: +65% brand affinity. CTA cards: +45% recall.
- Content proposition in the first 3 s. Captions at 5–10 words per second. 9:16, safe zone, lo-fi style, people on screen.
- Stimulation (pattern interrupts): fast music, transitions, scene changes, text pop-ups.

**YouTube ABCD (Google / Ipsos)**
- Attract (2+ shots in the first 5 s, people addressing the viewer), Brand (brand in the first 5 s, named by people on screen), Connect (emotional and functional messages, humour), Direct (specific CTA, urgency).
- +30% short-term sales likelihood, +17% long-term brand contribution.
- "Heartbeat" arc: start high, multiple peaks, brand cues throughout.
- 95% of YouTube video plays with sound on.

**YouTube Shorts**
- 9:16, under 60 s. Sound gives over 20% more conversions.

**Meta**
- 2017: 47% of a video's value in the first 3 s, 74% in the first 10 s; design for sound off.
- Reels: sound on is the default.
- Rule that satisfies both: copy must read with no sound; sound adds to it.

**UGC formats** (agency sources only)

| Format | Stages | Underlying formula |
|---|---|---|
| Problem–solution | Problem → agitate → failed alternative → product → result → CTA | PAS |
| Testimonial | Hook → before → discovery → results → CTA | BAB + proof |
| Before–after | Before → after → how → CTA | BAB |
| Unboxing | Mid-action open → reaction → reveal → why → CTA | AIDA |
| Listicle | Numbered hook → reasons → CTA | 4Ps / FAB |
| Day-in-the-life | Routine → product in context → payoff → soft CTA | Story / SB7 |

**Timing anchors:** proposition by 3 s (TikTok, Meta); brand by 5 s (YouTube); hook within 6 s (TikTok). Splits for 30 s and 60 s are unverified.

## Brand vs. performance (Binet & Field, 996 campaigns)
- Brand: long-term growth, broad reach, emotional, ~60% of budget.
- Performance: short-term sales, in-market buyers, rational (product, offer, urgency), ~40%.
- Emotional campaigns are almost twice as likely to give top-box profit growth.

## Synthesis (by the research agent)

**Properties every persuasive video must have:**
1. One big idea.
2. One target viewer with a known awareness level.
3. An existing desire or pain to channel.
4. Attention in the first 1–3 s.
5. Continuity: each beat earns the next.
6. A believable claim matched to market sophistication.
7. Specificity.
8. Proof.
9. Emotional driver plus rational justification.
10. Brand or product linkage.
11. One clear, specific desired action.
12. Platform-native legibility (9:16, safe zone, captions that work with no sound).

**Common roles:** R1 Hook, R2 Identify, R3 Tension, R4 Vision, R5 Mechanism, R6 Proof, R7 Offer, R8 Action. Brand is a layer across all roles.

| Formula | R1 Hook | R2 Identify | R3 Tension | R4 Vision | R5 Mechanism | R6 Proof | R7 Offer | R8 Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AIDA | Attention | Interest | — | Desire | (Interest/Desire) | — | — | Action |
| PAS | (Problem) | Problem | Agitate | — | Solution | — | — | (implied) |
| BAB | (Before) | Before | (Before) | After | Bridge | — | — | (implied) |
| 4Ps | Promise | — | — | Picture | — | Proof | — | Push |
| PASTOR | (Problem) | Problem | Amplify | Transformation | Story | Testimony | Offer | Response |
| FAB | — | — | — | Benefit | Feature, Advantage | — | — | — |
| Hook–Story–Offer | Hook | Story | Story | Story | Story | — | Offer | (Offer) |
| SB7 | — | Character + Problem | Failure | Success | Guide + Plan | Guide | — | CTA |
| YouTube ABCD | Attract | Connect | Connect | Connect | — | — | Direct | Direct |
| TikTok | Hook | Body | Body | Body | Body | Body | Close | Close |

- Core in almost every formula: Hook → Value (vision + mechanism) → Action.
- Optional roles (Identify, Tension, Proof, Offer) are switched on by awareness level and sophistication.

**What decides each property:**

| Property | Decided by |
|---|---|
| Opening content; which optional roles appear | Audience awareness level |
| Claim type | Market sophistication |
| Core desire | Audience owns it; product selects it |
| Big idea, mechanism, proof, offer, brand assets | Product / message |
| Length, aspect, pacing, sound, captions, CTA mechanics | Platform |
| Emotional vs. rational balance, CTA hardness | Objective (brand vs. performance) |
