# Research: Story Structure in Literature and Screenwriting

> Research input for [02-creative-director.md](../subsystems/02-creative-director.md). Collected 2026-10-04 by a research agent.
> Labels: **[V]** = confirmed by a fetched or searched source. **[unverified]** = craft knowledge, not confirmed in this research. Examples are illustrative unless a source is named.

## Frameworks

**Aristotle, *Poetics*** [V]
- A whole has a beginning, a middle and an end (VII).
- Unity of action: if a part is removed and nothing changes, the part is not needed (VIII).
- Causality: events happen *because of* earlier events, not only *after* them (X).
- Reversal (peripeteia): the action turns to its opposite. Recognition (anagnorisis): a change from ignorance to knowledge (XI).
- Plot first, character second (VI).

**Freytag's pyramid (1863)** [V]
- Exposition → rising action → climax → falling action → catastrophe.

**Three-act paradigm (Syd Field, 1979)** [V]
- Act I setup → Plot Point I → Act II confrontation (with a midpoint) → Plot Point II → Act III resolution.

**Save the Cat (Blake Snyder, 2005)** [V for the beat list; page targets approximate]
- Opening Image, Theme Stated, Set-up, Catalyst, Debate, Break into Two, B Story, Fun and Games, Midpoint, Bad Guys Close In, All Is Lost, Dark Night of the Soul, Break into Three, Finale, Final Image.
- The Final Image mirrors the Opening Image to show the change.

**Dan Harmon's Story Circle** [V]
- You → Need → Go → Search → Find → Take → Return → Change.

**Hero's journey (Campbell 1949; Vogler)** [V for Vogler's 12 stages]
- Ordinary World, Call, Refusal, Mentor, First Threshold, Tests/Allies/Enemies, Approach, Ordeal, Reward, Road Back, Resurrection, Return with the Elixir.

**Kishōtenketsu (起承転結)** [V, secondary sources]
- Ki (introduction) → Shō (development) → Ten (twist) → Ketsu (reconciliation).
- No protagonist-versus-antagonist conflict is needed.

**In medias res (Horace, *Ars Poetica*)** [V]
- Start in the middle of the action. Supply the backstory later.
- An ordering technique, not a structure.

**Booker, *The Seven Basic Plots* (2004)** [V, secondary]
- Overcoming the Monster, Rags to Riches, The Quest, Voyage and Return, Comedy, Tragedy, Rebirth.

**Polti, *The 36 Dramatic Situations* (1895)** [V]
- Each situation is a set of roles plus a relation. Example: Deliverance = an unfortunate, a threatener, a rescuer.
- A catalog of conflict configurations, not a sequence.

**Vonnegut's story shapes** [V]
- Axes: time and good/ill fortune. Shapes include Man in Hole, Boy Meets Girl, Cinderella.

**Reagan et al. (2016), "The emotional arcs of stories"** [V]
- 1,327 Project Gutenberg fiction texts, sentiment over time.
- Six arcs: rags to riches (rise), tragedy (fall), man in a hole (fall-rise), Icarus (rise-fall), Cinderella (rise-fall-rise), Oedipus (fall-rise-fall).

**McKee, *Story* (1997)** [V for the scene definition and inciting incident; gap concept from secondary sources]
- A scene turns a value-charged condition of a character's life on at least one value (+→– or –→+). If no value turns, the scene is a "nonevent".
- Beat: an action/reaction exchange inside a scene.
- Inciting incident: radically upsets the balance of the protagonist's life.
- Gap: the character expects a result, the world gives a different one. The gap drives the next action.
- Hierarchy: beat < scene < sequence < act < story [unverified in this research].

**Pixar Story Spine (Kenn Adams)** [V]
- Once upon a time… Every day… But one day… Because of that (×3)… Until finally… Ever since then…

**Story elements (craft consensus)** [mostly unverified as one list]
- Character, want vs. need, goal, stakes, conflict/obstacle, change/arc, setting, theme, point of view.

**Minimal stories** [V]
- Prince (1973): minimal story = state A → event → state B. B is caused by the event, differs from A, and relates to A.
- Forster (1927): "The king died and then the queen died" = story. "…of grief" = plot (causality).
- Narrativity is scalar (Prince, Ryan, Abbott). Ryan's conditions group as spatial (a world), temporal (change), mental (agents with intent), formal/pragmatic (closure and a point).
- Labov & Waletzky (1967): abstract, orientation, complicating action, evaluation, resolution, coda. Complicating action is the only obligatory part.
- Six-word story "For sale: baby shoes, never worn": the reader infers the change. Not by Hemingway (Quote Investigator).

**Non-narrative forms (Bordwell & Thompson, *Film Art*)** [form names V; detailed structure unverified]
- Categorical: topic → member examples.
- Rhetorical: claim → evidence → appeal → call to belief or action.
- Abstract: visual or audio qualities, theme and variation.
- Associational: juxtaposed images build a feeling or concept.

**Scene-level craft** [V]
- Swain: scene = goal → conflict → disaster. Sequel = reaction → dilemma → decision. The decision becomes the next goal.
- Parker & Stone: between beats say "but" or "therefore", never "and then".

## Synthesis (by the research agent)

**Minimum properties of every story:**
1. An initial state (world, subject, status quo).
2. An event or disruption in time.
3. A changed end state that differs from the start but relates to it.
4. Causal or meaningful connection ("because", not "and then").
5. A point: why it matters.
6. Unity: every part is needed.
- An agent, conflict and stakes raise narrativity but are not required.

**Common roles across frameworks:** R1 Orientation, R2 Disruption, R3 Pursuit/Development, R4 Turn/Crisis, R5 Resolution, R6 Meaning.

| Framework | R1 Orientation | R2 Disruption | R3 Development | R4 Turn | R5 Resolution | R6 Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aristotle | Beginning | start of action | Middle | Reversal + Recognition | End | — |
| Freytag | Exposition | Inciting force | Rising action | Climax | Falling action, Catastrophe | — |
| Field | Setup | Plot Point I | Confrontation, Midpoint | Plot Point II | Resolution | — |
| Save the Cat | Opening Image, Set-up | Catalyst … Break into Two | Fun & Games … Bad Guys Close In | All Is Lost … Break into Three | Finale | Theme Stated, Final Image |
| Harmon | You | Need, Go | Search, Find | Take | Return | Change |
| Vogler | Ordinary World | Call … Threshold | Tests, Approach | Ordeal … Resurrection | Return | Elixir |
| Kishōtenketsu | Ki | (none) | Shō | Ten | Ketsu | Ketsu |
| Story Spine | Every day | But one day | Because of that ×3 | Until finally | Ever since then | (implied) |
| Labov | Abstract, Orientation | Complicating action | Complicating action | peak | Resolution, Coda | Evaluation |
| Prince | State A | Event | — | — | State B | — |
| Swain | Goal | Conflict | Conflict | Disaster | Reaction, Dilemma, Decision | — |
| McKee | Balance | Inciting incident | Complications | Crisis, Climax | Resolution | Value change |

- Taxonomies (Booker, Polti, Reagan) are a different axis. They classify the content and the shape of the change, not the stage sequence.
- Parameters: arc shape, conflict type, telling order (linear or in medias res), conflict present or absent.

**Kinds of story:**

| Kind | Keeps | Drops | Video example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classical narrative | R1–R6 | — | short film |
| Non-conflict narrative (Kishōtenketsu) | R1, R3, R4 (twist), R5, R6 | conflict | slice-of-life clip |
| Minimal narrative | R1, one event, R5 | development chain | 5 s animated photo |
| Oral / anecdotal (Labov) | R1, R2/R3, R5, R6 | formal crisis | testimonial |
| Categorical | topic, list, summary | causality, agent | product feature reel |
| Rhetorical | problem, evidence, call to action, meaning | character arc | ad, explainer |
| Abstract | progression, variation, closure | agent, causality | motion-graphics loop |
| Associational / lyric | mood, rising intensity | causality, agent | mood piece, music video |

- Common to all kinds: progression from a start state to an end state that differs in a felt or understood way [synthesis, unverified as a published claim].

**Scenes:**
- A scene is a state transition: input state → action against opposition → output state, with a perceptible turn (McKee, Swain).
- If output value = input value, the scene is a nonevent. Cut or merge it.
- Fractal: beats, scenes, sequences and acts have the same shape.
- Edges between scenes: "therefore" or "but" in narrative. "And also", "like / against", "variation of" in non-narrative forms.
- Data-model sketch (agent suggestion, unverified): `Scene = {state_in, event, opposition?, state_out, value_axis, delta, edge_to_next}`.
