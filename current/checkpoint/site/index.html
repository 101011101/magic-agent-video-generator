In the cottage beneath the tower, she places the shell beside a chipped cup on the kitchen table. Its glow persists through the fading day, a small warmth among worn wood, faded blue walls and neatly mended clothes. She eats alone, occasionally looking toward it.

At night, the shell begins to hum—a low, trembling thread within the musical score. Beyond the window, a storm gathers. Rain obscures the sea, and the lighthouse lamp goes out.

Far offshore, several ships drift toward the dark reef.

The keeper takes the shell and climbs the tower. Wind presses rain through the narrow windows. She grips the iron railing, ascending the worn steps with the shell held against her chest. Its light spills between her fingers.

At the top, she stands beside the extinguished lamp. She opens its glass housing and brings the shell close to the dark burner. Amber light gathers there, faint at first, then bright enough to illuminate her face. The lamp catches.

Its beam returns across the storm-dark water. The keeper watches it pass over the ships. One changes course, then another; their lights move steadily away from the reef toward open sea. She stays beside the lamp until they are safe.

Morning comes softly. The storm has left scattered weed and driftwood along the beach.

The keeper returns to the place where she found the shell. She kneels and settles it into the wet sand. Its glow is faint now, almost lost in the dawn.

Her hand lingers beside it before she stands. She walks back toward the lighthouse as a thin sheet of water reaches the shell. The score fades, and the returning tide leaves its spiral resting in the pale morning light.

Pipeline

  1. Creative director (LLM): the prompt becomes a story, then change-based operational chunks (baseline + property: from → to per chunk).
  2. Shot planner (1 LLM call): a bible (each character, prop and location with a fixed look; one style line) and a shot list. A shot is one action, 5 or 10 s, a fixed camera or one simple move, linked to the previous shot as cut or continuous. Uploads and on-screen text are marked here.
  3. References: one clean image per bible entry that appears in 2 or more scenes (nano-banana-pro 2k). An uploaded image is used as is.
  4. Cut start frames: image editor with the references (or the upload itself when the request is “animate this photo”). A vision check counts the entities; a frame with a missing or doubled character is made again once.
  5. Clips: wan-3.0, 720p, start frame only, no end frame. A continuous shot starts from the real last frame of the previous clip; a vision check confirms its characters are in that frame.
  6. Stitch: native 30 fps, 1280×720, hard joins. Exact text is rendered separately (headless browser) and overlaid, so it is never misspelled.

Jobs are cached by a hash of their prompt and input files, so a change re-runs only what depends on it. --estimate prices a run before it spends anything.

Known issues

  • Cut frames are drawn from isolated references and text, not from earlier frames, so scale and context are re-invented at every cut (the shell is often too large; the lighthouse lamp reads as a hand lantern).
  • When a character leaves the frame in a continuous chain, the video model can invent a new person when the next action needs them (a check and frame repair now guard this).
  • No audio yet (score, sound, voice).
  • The grandma photo is a stand-in; real uploads are not tested yet.
  • Next: build each cut by transforming the earlier frame that already holds the most of its elements, and merge missing ones from the frames where they last appeared (in test).
