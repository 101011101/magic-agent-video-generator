**Sweet Crumbs** — a 20-second ad

It is still dark outside, just before seven. Inside Sweet Crumbs, a single row of warm pendant lights glows over a flour-dusted wooden counter. The ovens hum. A baker in a cream apron presses her palms into a soft round of dough, folding it over itself with practiced, easy movements. Flour lifts into the air and hangs in the light like fine snow.

An oven door swings open and a wave of heat rolls out. Inside, a tray of croissants has turned deep golden, their layers puffed and crackling. Beside them, a loaf of sourdough splits along its scored top, the crust blistered and dark at the edges. The baker slides the tray out and sets it on the cooling rack. Steam rises from everything.

Morning arrives. Pale blue light fills the front windows. The display case is now full: glossy fruit tarts, cinnamon rolls swirled with white icing, rows of cookies with crisp edges, a tall chocolate cake with a single slice already missing. The bell above the door rings. A woman steps in from the street, pulls off her scarf, and breathes in. Her shoulders drop. She smiles before she has even reached the counter.

At a small table by the window, a father and his young daughter share a cinnamon roll. The girl tears off the soft center, and a thin ribbon of icing stretches between her fingers. She laughs. Her father brushes a crumb from her cheek. Outside, the street is waking up, but in here everything feels slow and warm.

The light settles. The bakery's warm tones soften behind a clean end card. The text appears, crisp and centered, spelled exactly:

**Sweet Crumbs — Open 7am–3pm, 12 Elm St**

The card holds for the final seconds, and the ad ends at twenty seconds.
