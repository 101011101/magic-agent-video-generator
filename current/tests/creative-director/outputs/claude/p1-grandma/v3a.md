**Grandma's Wave** — a 5-second moment

Afternoon sun pours through the living-room window and settles in a warm stripe across the armchair. Dust drifts slowly in the light. Grandma sits as she always does, deep in the cushions, her short white hair soft and bright where the sun catches it. Her round glasses hold two small reflections of the window. Her green cardigan is buttoned to the middle, the wool slightly fuzzy at the cuffs.

For a heartbeat she is still, looking straight ahead with that calm, neutral expression of someone waiting for the photo to be over. Then something changes. The corners of her eyes crinkle first, behind the glasses, and the smile follows — slow and genuine, lifting her cheeks and softening her whole face. It is the smile of someone who has just recognized a person she loves.

Her right hand rises from the arm of the chair. It lifts to about shoulder height, palm open, and she waves — two or three gentle side-to-side movements, unhurried, the way grandmothers wave from a doorway. Her head tilts very slightly as she does it. The cardigan sleeve slides a little down her wrist.

She holds the smile as the wave settles, her hand pausing in the air for a final second. The room stays quiet and golden around her. Nothing else moves except the drifting dust and the warmth in her face.

The whole moment lasts about five seconds — a small, tender greeting from her chair, as if she has just seen you walk in.
