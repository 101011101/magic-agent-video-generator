# Grandma Smiles and Waves — 5-Second Photo Animation

**Source:** Your photo (grandma in the armchair, green cardigan, sunlit living room)
**Length:** 5 seconds
**Aspect ratio:** Match the original photo
**Camera:** Locked off. No zoom or pan, so it feels like the photo itself came to life.
**Audio:** None by default (optional: soft room tone or a gentle piano note)

---

### 0.0s – 1.0s — The photo breathes
Grandma sits exactly as in the photo, looking at the camera with her neutral expression. Subtle signs of life only: a slow breath lifts her shoulders slightly, and she blinks once. Sunlight in the room stays still and warm.

### 1.0s – 2.2s — The smile
Her eyes soften first. The corners of her eyes crinkle behind her round glasses, then a warm, closed-mouth smile spreads across her face. Her head tilts very slightly, as if she just recognized someone she loves.

### 2.2s – 4.2s — The wave
She lifts her right hand from the arm of the chair to about shoulder height, palm facing the camera, and gives two or three slow, gentle waves. The movement is relaxed and natural for her age, not quick or bouncy. The sleeve of her green cardigan moves with her arm. Her smile grows a little wider, with a hint of teeth.

### 4.2s – 5.0s — Settle
Her hand lowers back toward the armrest. She holds the smile and keeps looking into the camera as the clip ends.

---

### Animation prompt (for the image-to-video model)
> The elderly woman in the photo comes to life. She breathes gently and blinks, then her eyes crinkle and she breaks into a warm, loving smile. She slowly raises her right hand and waves gently at the camera two or three times, then lowers her hand while still smiling. Natural, subtle, realistic motion. Static camera. Keep her face, white hair, round glasses, green cardigan, armchair and sunlit living room exactly as in the original photo.

**Negative prompt:** distorted face, changing identity, extra fingers, warped hands, morphing glasses, fast or jerky movement, camera movement, talking, background changes

### Notes for a good result
- Keep the movement small. With real family photos, a subtle animation looks more like *her*; big movements tend to change the face.
- Hands are the most likely failure point. If the wave looks wrong, regenerate, or try a version where she only raises her hand slightly from the armrest.
- If you want, I can also make a version with a slow push-in on her face, or a looping version for sharing.
