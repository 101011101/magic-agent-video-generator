At dawn, the beach lies beneath a wash of pearl-grey light. Pools between the rocks hold the pale sky; ribbons of kelp mark the retreating tide. Above the shore stands a weathered lighthouse, its white walls stained by salt.

The keeper walks alone along the waterline, her coat hanging heavily from narrow shoulders. Among the broken shells, something glows.

She kneels. A small spiral shell rests in the sand, lit from within by a soft amber radiance. She turns it in her palm, and its light warms her weathered fingers. For a moment she remains there, reluctant to disturb it. Then she carries it home.

In the cottage beneath the tower, she places the shell beside a chipped cup on the kitchen table. Its glow persists through the fading day, a small warmth among worn wood, faded blue walls and neatly mended clothes. She eats alone, occasionally looking toward it.

At night, the shell begins to hum—a low, trembling thread within the musical score. Beyond the window, a storm gathers. Rain obscures the sea, and the lighthouse lamp goes out.

Far offshore, several ships drift toward the dark reef.

The keeper takes the shell and climbs the tower. Wind presses rain through the narrow windows. She grips the iron railing, ascending the worn steps with the shell held against her chest. Its light spills between her fingers.

At the top, she stands beside the extinguished lamp. She opens its glass housing and brings the shell close to the dark burner. Amber light gathers there, faint at first, then bright enough to illuminate her face. The lamp catches.

Its beam returns across the storm-dark water. The keeper watches it pass over the ships. One changes course, then another; their lights move steadily away from the reef toward open sea. She stays beside the lamp until they are safe.

Morning comes softly. The storm has left scattered weed and driftwood along the beach.

The keeper returns to the place where she found the shell. She kneels and settles it into the wet sand. Its glow is faint now, almost lost in the dawn.

Her hand lingers beside it before she stands. She walks back toward the lighthouse as a thin sheet of water reaches the shell. The score fades, and the returning tide leaves its spiral resting in the pale morning light.
