Three grid prompts, same scene

The counter scene, loaves only, nano-banana-pro at 4k, one image per test, 300 credits each. Every prompt is quoted in full below and in prompts.txt. Each test shows the grid as drawn, then its nine cells as one strip in reading order.

A. Text direct to grid

The scene (setting, people, what happens) and "turn it into a grid of 9". No per-cell lines, no images.

  • It directs its own commercial: a richer bakery with a display case and chalkboards, close-ups in the last row, a cut to the doorway. Looks like an ad; is not a continuous shot from one camera.
  • The cast is its own: a bearded baker in a white tee, an older man in a green jacket, a woman in a brown coat, a family at the end. Nobody from the description (no silver bob and glasses, no moustache, no sky-blue jumper).
  • The story is told twice: a loaf wrapped and handed over to one customer in cells 1–3, again to another in 4–6, then a smile, a family, a doorway.

Turn this scene into a grid of 9 consecutive frames: 3 rows × 3 columns, equal cells, edge to edge, no gutters, borders, numbers or text, each cell a live-action photograph from one fixed camera, in reading order.
The scene: A live-action photograph, cinema camera, shallow depth of field, warm practical light. Inside a small neighbourhood bakery at opening time, from one fixed wide camera in the rear customer-side corner: a blue glazed front door at the far left with a chest-height shop sign on it, a wooden sales counter on the left running from near to far, a wooden workbench with a cream flour bowl at the far right under a large front window, a black baking oven and a wire cooling rack against the right wall, cream plaster walls, honey-coloured timber, worn stone floor. Behind the counter stands the baker: a middle-aged man with warm tan skin, a short brown moustache, short wavy dark-brown hair, a cream shirt with the sleeves rolled up, a honey-tan bib apron dusted with flour, midnight-blue trousers. Across the counter stand three customers: nearest the door, the neighbour, an older woman with a chin-length silver-grey bob, small round brown-framed glasses, a soft-blue cardigan over a cream blouse, a calf-length terracotta skirt, a plain cream canvas shopping bag on her arm; beside her the parent, a young woman with medium-brown skin and dark curly hair in a low bun, a terracotta hip-length jacket over a butter-yellow shirt, deep-blue trousers; beside the parent the child, about five, medium-brown skin, short springy dark curls, a sky-blue jumper over a cream shirt, honey-ochre trousers. Two baked oval loaves with scored tops rest side by side on the cooling rack; a stack of plain cream wrapping paper lies on the counter in front of the baker. Nothing else: no other bread or pastries, no other people. At the counter, the baker wraps a warm loaf in paper and hands it to the neighbour, then wraps the second loaf for the parent. The baker's tired face brightens at their grateful smiles.

B. The scene prompt as cell 1, then one slug per cell

Each later cell is stated on its own: who holds what, how many loaves on the rack, who is there. No images.

  • Eight of nine cells are their slug: two loaves on the rack → one lifted, one left → wrapped → handed to the neighbour → she holds hers, rack empty → second wrapped → handed to the parent → both hold theirs, baker's head down → baker smiles, child looking up. One fixed camera throughout. The best sequence of any test so far.
  • Cell 9 grows two extra loaves on the counter. The rack became a wall-mounted wire panel with the loaves hung on it, and a second rack on the counter.
  • The people are consistent across cells but only half from the description: moustache yes, glasses no, a floral blouse, a ginger child with pale skin instead of dark curls and medium-brown skin, a grey apron over a tee instead of the cream shirt and tan apron.

C. The first frame given as image 1, cell 1 = image 1, eight changes

Image 1 is the first frame from the earlier first-and-last test (the grid's label cropped off). "Everything stays exactly the same as image 1 except for these changes", then eight numbered changes.

Image 1 as sent:

  • Cell 1 is image 1, near enough to be the same frame. The four people are exactly the people of image 1 in every cell where they appear: identity is solved by the anchor.
  • "Everything stays the same as image 1" beat the changes on the rack: the two loaves never leave it (cells 2, 4, 5, 9 still show both) although the changes say one, then none. The anchor image outranks a sentence that contradicts it.
  • Cells 3 and 6 are close-ups of the hands wrapping, although every cell was to be from the camera of image 1. Cell 8 loses the neighbour out of frame.
  • Otherwise the order is right: lifted, wrapped, handed to the neighbour, second loaf, wrapped, handed to the parent, head down, smiling with everyone back in cell 9.

D. Only the scene description, nothing else

The story's own lines for the scene and "make a grid of 9". No room, no character descriptions, no images. 300 credits.

  • One camera in all nine cells, no close-ups, one room; its own cast (a woman baker, a grey-haired neighbour, a father with a toddler) kept consistent throughout.
  • The story reads in order: the loaf wrapped and handed to the neighbour (1–3), the parent and child arrive and the second loaf is wrapped and handed over (4–6, 9), the baker's smile (7–8).
  • It staged the two customers one after the other instead of together, and the loaves on the counter vary from cell to cell (two to five). With fewer instructions it had less to contradict; it also had less to be right about.
