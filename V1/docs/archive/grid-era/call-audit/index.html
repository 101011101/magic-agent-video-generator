Pipeline audit: model calls and prompts

Scope: the scene pipeline (scenes.mjs → scene-video.mjs) as run on the bakery ad, read from src/*.js and from the prompts recorded in runs/custom-bakery-real/run.json. The older per-slide path (run.mjs: blocking, verify-step, motion) is noted at the end. Counts are for one run of 6 scenes, 27 panels, a 208-word story, 4 characters, 2 places.

3. Inefficient calls

F1. 49 calls to find empty cells

Every 3×3 grid with spare cells gets one vision call per cell (cell-occupied: "Is any person or animal visible?"), 9 per attempt, to skip the cells the model left empty. grid-check already looks at the whole grid once; an empty_cells field there costs nothing. (Code alternative: SSIM of each cell against the plate; the 49 cached answers can validate it for free.)

F2. 27–45 cell checks whose result is discarded

panel-check counts every id in every cell (including "flour dusting the apron" and items packed in others). A missing or duplicated element only sets forced: true on the frame; the cell is kept regardless. Meanwhile the one check that would have caught scene 5's wrong baker and wrong customers, identity against the sheets, is not run in the grid path (it exists in verify.js for the old path). Replace with one call per cell that compares the cell with the sheets of the characters present (identity), counts only characters, and says same room / same camera against the plate; drop the separate room check. Fewer calls, and a result that can send a cell back.

F3. The ID section: 15–20 sequential calls

Six story chunks → six ids-tag calls in series (each carries the running table, so they cannot run in parallel), each followed by a coverage call and a re-tag when something was missed, then classify, merge, link-propose and one link-check per pair. About five minutes of wall time per solve. One call that rewrites the story with one unique name per thing and returns the glossary (name → id, role, part-of, count), checked by code for pronouns and unknown nouns, replaces tag, coverage and merge; classify and links fold into the glossary.

F4. The ground plan writes what nothing reads

seed-plan (high effort, the longest call) returns for every mark where, essential, area, size, faces, key plus a scale list and light. The plate prompt reads name and essential; the storyboard reads essential; key is overwritten by code (makeSeed); size, faces, scale are read by nothing (only the report page shows size). For the shop it wrote 20 marks (9,100 characters), eight of them sub-parts: a door handle, the sign's status strip, the oven chamber, "Workbench working surface", "Cooling surface", "Counter service surface". Those sub-parts then appear in the plate prompt (15 facts), in every plate observation (20 questions, 12 of which cannot fail the plate) and in every storyboard prompt (15-item layout line per environment).

F5. Painter's specs for things that get one line of use

asset-spec at high effort writes 2–4 sentences for all 14 tracked things. Only the 4 characters get sheets; each prop spec is used once, as a text reference the first time the prop appears, and never again. The detail invites invention (the loaf's "three diagonal expansion marks", "darker toasted-brown lower edge"). A prop needs one line: shape, size, material, colour.

F6. Serialised retries

Sheet first tries run in parallel, but every retry runs in a sequential loop per asset (makeAssets): the neighbour's six retries (≈ 45 s each plus a check) held the run for about ten minutes. Link checks run one pair at a time. Both are trivially parallel.

F7. Half the credits are retries, and the retry prompts cannot work

Every retry sends a fresh generation with a note about a previous attempt the model never sees: "The last try got this wrong; fix it: plain flat light-grey background", "The last attempt broke the rule in these cells; fix them: cell 1 changed the camera". The generator has no memory; the note must restate the rule in positive terms, not refer to history. Two of the baker's three tries and two of the neighbour's were spent on a floor shadow the check later learned to accept.

F8. The old path is still in the tree

run.mjs, block/block-fix/block-patch, verify-step (one call per slide with up to 8 sheet images at medium effort), motion, world, the question loop. Most entries in .cache are from it, the PRD still describes it, and two stages (style-medium, panel-check) exist twice with different prompts. Quarantine or delete it.

4. Badly made prompts (quoted as sent)

P1. Two or three accounts of every cell

4. Baker lifts the loaf from the cooling rack. (baker: lifts the loaf from the cooling rack; loaf: rises from the rack in baker's hands)
1. Baker lifts a croissant from the workbench tray while neighbour, parent and child wait across the counter. [baker between the counter and the workbench, lifting a croissant from the pastry tray; flour dusting the apron on the baker's apron, dusting the baker's apron; neighbour at the counter nearer the blue front door, standing across the counter from the baker; canvas shopping bag at the neighbour's side, hanging from the neighbour's arm; … loaf on the cooling rack near the right wall, resting on the cooling surface] (baker: lifts a croissant from the tray; croissant handed to the neighbour: rises from the tray in baker's hand)

The action sentence, the bracketed placement list and the parenthesised change list say the same thing three ways with different wording. Tested on scene 5: one account (cell 1 in full, later cells only their change) kept the customers in 7 of 8 cells against 3 of 8. Deltas should be the resulting state, which is what the state table holds.

P2. The storyboard is shaped for the edit chain

Its instruction says "The picture is edited from the previous panel, so describe the change, not the whole picture", and its schema gives an element three ways to be sourced (enters with where/doing, changes with now, introductions with a beat), all three accepted by the hard rule. Grids draw every cell from the plate; cell 1 needs the full state, every later cell one delta. The schema should be: panel 1 = full state (every element, where, doing); panels 2+ = now per changed element, leaves, and the beat each panel covers.

P3. "Storybook" in every stage, whatever the medium

Live-action photographic footage like a real television commercial … no illustration, no painting.. One single image …
Camera: … A full, finished storybook painting of a lived-in place, with these facts:

That is the shop plate prompt of the photoreal run: it forbids painting and asks for a painting in the same breath. Also: sheets ("Reference sheet of ONE character", which produced a figurine seven times), seed-observe ("quality: 5 = a finished storybook page", with a retry note "paint a full, finished storybook page, not a sparse sketch"), asset-set ("reference sheets of one illustrated story"), storyboard and sound ("animated storybook film"), screenplay ("for an animated film"), story ("Write a short illustrated story"), checks ("a frame from an illustrated story"). The test on the neighbour showed the medium word decides the result when it is the noun of the sentence. One medium object, derived once ("a studio photograph of a real person" / "a 3D-rendered character design" / "a watercolour illustration of"), should be the noun in sheets, plates, grids and checks.

P4. Story state written into identity

loaf: A broad, oval loaf with rounded ends, a honey-gold crust, and three diagonal expansion marks … Plain cream wrapping paper cups its base …
pastry tray: … It holds six plump, unbaked butter-yellow croissants …
paper bag: … It contains one plump honey-gold croissant …
shop-sign: … reads "The Corner Oven", with "Come in. Your morning starts here." beneath it
shop-sign.status: Initially OPEN toward the camera

The spec call is told "never later states" and then told, for every container, "At the start it holds: … (draw them in it)" (from the ID pass's inside field). The loaf got its wrapping paper that way, the tray its raw croissants, the bag its croissant. The ground plan put the final tagline on the sign from the first frame and set the status strip to OPEN before the baker turns it. Specs and plans must describe invariants; state belongs to panels.

P5. References numbered per element, images sent per file

pastry tray: exactly as it looks in image 3 (take only pastry tray from it)
loaf: exactly as it looks in image 4 (take only loaf from it)          ← inputs: 3 images

P6. Retry notes that instruct the wrong thing

The last try got this wrong; fix it: ; match the other sheets: Stylized 3D cartoon rendering with an enlarged head, oversized eyes and simplified textures, unlike the photorealistic majority.

The set check's note describes the offender; the code pastes it after "match the other sheets:", so four tries were told to draw a cartoon. The check should return what the sheet must become.

P7. The storyboard fix pass is a fake beat

  12. (Storyboard note: fix these: scene 4 panel 3: parent has no source …)

Violations are appended to the scene's beat list as an action beat; the model is asked to storyboard a note. They belong in the instruction.

P8. ID noise reaches every prompt

flour dusting the apron on the baker's apron, dusting the baker's apron
croissant handed to the neighbour: rises from the tray in baker's hand

"Flour dusting the apron" was classified as an asset, so it is an element of every panel with its own where/doing, counted in every cell, and specified ("Fine, flour-white powder forming irregular, thin patches…"). "Croissant handed to the neighbour" is an id name used as a noun. The glossary rewrite fixes both (a name is a noun phrase; surface detail is not a thing).

P9. The clip prompt

Baker pulls the oven door down toward us as steam rolls out of the opening. pulls down the oven door; grip the lowered oven door's handle. Natural motion only; … Sound: Low oven hum and faint birdsong outside.; creak and click of the oven door opening, brief hiss of escaping steam; no voices; no music.

changes[].now phrases are glued on without subjects; the sfx of every beat the panel covers are joined; "no music" is hard-coded although screenplay-sound wrote a music cue per scene; "no voices" even for dialogue panels (the dialogue branch exists but has not been exercised). A clip prompt is: the start state in one clause, the one action, the sound of that action, and the scene's ambience.

P10. The screenplay adds actions

Three story sentences for the counter scene became eleven beats, including "lifts a croissant from the tray", "lifts the loaf from the cooling rack", "shoulders droop", "lifts their head, eyes opening wider". "Add nothing that changes the plot" permits it. Each beat should quote the story words it comes from; a beat with no source is not allowed. The headings also contradict themselves: "INT. counter — CONTINUOUS (after a cut)".

P11. The story call does not know what it is writing

"Write a short illustrated story … 150–300 words" is the whole brief, for a request that said "a 30-second video ad". It gets no medium, format, length in seconds or number of scenes; its style line is then overwritten by --style. The brief should carry the deliverable (ad, 30 s, ends on the name and a line) and the medium.

P12. Checks that cannot be answered as asked

seed-observe asks "visible / holds / observed_where" for sub-part marks ("Counter service surface: Clear upper surface at waist height"); panel-check asks for a count of flour and of croissants inside a tray; asset-check has no "is it a photograph" question although that was the only thing wrong.
