The Twice-Seen Scarab: elements first, then two referenced clips

Three kinds of call. 1: one LLM call writes the scene, gives every physical thing an id and a spec, and fills a presence table (which ids are in which clip). 2: one image call per element makes its reference sheet before any video. 3: one text-to-video call per clip (seedance-2.0, 480p, 8 s) whose references are exactly that clip's elements, named @id in the prompt; clip 2 also gets clip 1's hand-off frame as @last_frame. 2936 credits so far. Code: seedream-test/run.mjs, outputs in seedream-test/runs/t1/.

1. The script (one LLM call)

Ivo Rusk crouches in the Sand Chamber beside the Low Arch. He rakes the Sand Bank apart and hauls the Sun Scarab completely out, revealing it. He thrusts the Sun Scarab into the Chest Satchel and snaps it shut. The disturbed Sand Bank slumps forward, burying Ivo Rusk to the waist. He twists, digs furiously, then seizes the Low Arch and heaves. Still pinned, Ivo Rusk rises slightly, clearly visible and straining mid-pull.

Without a break, Ivo Rusk heaves again and claws himself completely free of the Sand Bank. He ducks through the Low Arch into the adjoining Escape Gallery, the Chest Satchel still closed against him. Only after clearing the Low Arch does he wrench the Chest Satchel open. He pulls the Sun Scarab entirely out and raises it toward the camera, revealing it again. Ivo Rusk keeps moving deeper into the Escape Gallery with the Sun Scarab plainly visible.

What happened

Clip 1 (references: the six elements in its column)

  • The hero is the man from the sheet: skin, white forelock, mustard shirt, rust neckerchief, chest satchel, all through both clips. This is the first run where identity came from a sheet, not from a frame, and it held.
  • The chamber is the plate: pale sandstone, roof slot, the sand bank in the rear corner. The scarab comes out of the sand at about 1.5 s (reveal one) and goes into the satchel at 2–3.5 s; the flap is closed by 4 s.
  • He is buried to the waist and digs (4.5–6 s).
  • The last two seconds cut to a new camera: a wide shot from outside the arch, the hero tiny and face-down in the sand far from the arch. The prompt said "fixed medium-wide camera" and "end mid-heave gripping the arch". Seedance read the long action list as a multi-shot scene, as it did in the adventure chain. The hand-off frame is therefore a wide shot with a small, turned-away hero.
  • He sits in the sand from frame 0 rather than crouching beside it, so "the sand bank slumps and buries him" is more "he is already in it".

Clip 2 (references: all seven elements plus @last_frame)

  • It starts from the hand-off: the same wide view through the arch, the hero in the sand. SSIM against the hand-off is only 0.578 because a reference is not a first frame: the model re-rendered the view (wider arch, slightly different wall texture) rather than copying pixels. As a cut it reads as continuous.
  • Every must-show fact lands: he crawls free (0–2.5 s), comes through the arch (3–4 s) from the sandstone chamber into the blue-grey basalt gallery, which is the gallery plate, opens the satchel (4.5–6 s), takes the scarab out and raises it to camera (6.5–8 s), reveal two. Same man, same clothes.
  • The room transition is the clearest in any run so far: two different plates, both recognisable, joined by the arch.
  • The scarab is a plain gold oval in both clips; the incised wing markings from the spec are not visible at 480p. The scarab sheet was a four-panel reveal sequence, not one object (see below), so the model had a weak reference for it.

The three calls

  • The API enforces the presence table. Clip 1's prompt mentioned @gallery ("remains hidden") while gallery was not in clip 1's column; Magic Hour refused the call with "No reference was provided for @gallery" before charging. The runner now demotes any @id outside the clip's column to its plain name. Clip 2's prompt must contain @last_frame when that reference is sent; it refused that too until the prompt kept it.
  • A PNG frame cut straight from the clip (864×496) was refused as a reference with a generic invalid_input error after the job was created (refunded). The same frame as a 1280×720 JPEG was accepted. Reference images need ordinary dimensions and formats.
  • Sheet specs that describe a state draw the state: the scarab spec said "begins completely buried", so its sheet is a four-panel sequence of it emerging from sand. The bakery notes found the same with the loaf. A sheet spec must be the thing alone, at rest.
  • The style line "cinema camera" put a film camera on a tripod into the chamber plate. Style words that name objects become objects on a plate.
  • The chamber plate's "open roof" became a modern steel-framed skylight; both clips kept it.

Against the adventure chain (last frame only, no sheets): identity now survives a hand-off in which the hero is small and turned away, because every call carries his sheet. The remaining failure is the one the grids had: a long ordered action list in one 8 s call gets re-staged with extra cuts, and the hand-off frame is whatever the model chose to end on. Next: (1) one or two beats per clip and more clips, so each prompt is short; (2) state the end framing as a must ("last frame: medium shot, hero gripping the arch, facing camera"); (3) fix the sheet rules (thing at rest, no camera words) and add the sheet check from the asset bank.
