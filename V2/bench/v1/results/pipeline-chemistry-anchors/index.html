1 · The brief

Make a 60-second widescreen explainer video on the citric acid cycle (the Krebs cycle) for first-year biology students. Show the cycle as a loop inside a mitochondrion: acetyl-CoA enters and joins oxaloacetate to form citrate, two carbons leave as CO2, NADH, FADH2 and ATP are produced along the way, and oxaloacetate is regenerated so the loop starts again. Label the key molecules on screen as they appear, under the title 'Citric acid cycle'. Calm English narration explaining each step as it happens. Simple clean 2D animation, no music.

length asked: 60 s · tier: free · video model: minimax-h3 @ 480p

2 · The story stage: gate, research, story

Gate: nonfiction, research needed (confidence no research needed: 0) · This factual biology explainer requires accurate biochemical steps, molecule labels, energy yields, and mitochondrial localization.

research: 10 facts, 6 looks
1. In eukaryotic cells, the cycle occurs in the mitochondrial matrix. Succinate dehydrogenase is the exception to the soluble cycle enzymes: it is embedded in the inner membrane. ([openstax.org](https://openstax.org/books/biology/pages/7-3-oxidation-of-pyruvate-and-the-citric-acid-cycle))
2. Acetyl-CoA supplies a two-carbon acetyl group, which joins four-carbon oxaloacetate to form six-carbon citrate. Coenzyme A is released; the whole acetyl-CoA molecule does not become citrate. ([openstax.org](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/7-3-oxidation-of-pyruvate-and-the-citric-acid-cycle))
3. Citrate is rearranged into isocitrate, retaining six carbons. This supplies the next labeled intermediate before carbon dioxide release begins. ([openstax.org](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/7-3-oxidation-of-pyruvate-and-the-citric-acid-cycle))
4. Isocitrate becomes five-carbon α-ketoglutarate, releasing one CO₂ and producing the first NADH. ([openstax.org](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/7-3-oxidation-of-pyruvate-and-the-citric-acid-cycle))
5. α-Ketoglutarate becomes four-carbon succinyl-CoA. A second CO₂ leaves, and a second NADH is produced. ([openstax.org](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/7-3-oxidation-of-pyruvate-and-the-citric-acid-cycle))
6. Succinyl-CoA becomes succinate, releasing CoA and generating one GTP or ATP, depending on cell type. For the requested ATP display, a GTP → ATP callout matches the textbook diagram without implying both are additional net products. ([openstax.org](https://openstax.org/books/biology/pages/7-3-oxidation-of-pyruvate-and-the-citric-acid-cycle))
7. Succinate becomes fumarate as FAD is reduced to FADH₂. Fumarate then becomes malate through addition of water. ([openstax.org](https://openstax.org/books/biology/pages/7-3-oxidation-of-pyruvate-and-the-citric-acid-cycle))
8. Malate becomes oxaloacetate, generating the third NADH. Regenerated oxaloacetate can accept another acetyl group, completing the loop. ([openstax.org](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/7-3-oxidation-of-pyruvate-and-the-citric-acid-cycle))
9. Each turn yields two CO₂, three NADH, one FADH₂ and one ATP equivalent. NADH and FADH₂ connect the cycle to subsequent ATP production through the electron-transport pathway. ([openstax.org](https://openstax.org/books/concepts-biology/pages/4-3-citric-acid-cycle-and-oxidative-phosphorylation?utm_source=openai))
10. Important animation safeguard: the two carbons released as CO₂ during the first turn are not the newly arriving acetyl carbons. Do not highlight two incoming carbon dots and immediately animate those same dots leaving; acetyl carbons are released on later turns. ([openstax.org](https://openstax.org/books/concepts-biology/pages/4-3-citric-acid-cycle-and-oxidative-phosphorylation?utm_source=openai))

Story: Citric acid cycle (explainer) · style: 60-second widescreen explainer in simple, clean 2D animation using textbook-style diagrams; calm English narration; no music.

the story text (1077 chars)
Under “Citric acid cycle,” an oval mitochondrial cutaway shows a symbolic, arrow-linked loop in the matrix. Labels identify molecules as calm English narration follows the reactions. Acetyl-CoA supplies a two-carbon acetyl group to four-carbon oxaloacetate, forming six-carbon citrate; CoA-SH is released.

Citrate rearranges into six-carbon isocitrate. Isocitrate becomes five-carbon α-ketoglutarate: CO₂ leaves and the first NADH appears. Next, four-carbon succinyl-CoA forms, releasing a second CO₂ and producing a second NADH. The departing carbons are not the newly arrived acetyl carbons.

Succinyl-CoA becomes succinate, releasing CoA-SH; a GTP → ATP callout represents one ATP equivalent, not two additional products. Membrane-embedded succinate dehydrogenase converts succinate to fumarate as FAD becomes FADH₂. Water enters as fumarate becomes malate.

Malate becomes oxaloacetate, producing the third NADH. The regenerated oxaloacetate can accept another acetyl group, closing the loop. The completed turn shows two CO₂, three NADH, one FADH₂ and one ATP equivalent.
