BRIEF

Make a 60-second widescreen explainer video on the citric acid cycle (the Krebs cycle) for first-year biology students. Show the cycle as a loop inside a mitochondrion: acetyl-CoA enters and joins oxaloacetate to form citrate, two carbons leave as CO2, NADH, FADH2 and ATP are produced along the way, and oxaloacetate is regenerated so the loop starts again. Label the key molecules on screen as they appear, under the title 'Citric acid cycle'. Calm English narration explaining each step as it happens. Simple clean 2D animation, no music.

mode: nonfiction · This is a factual biology explainer requiring accurate reaction steps, molecule labels, energy yields, and mitochondrial localization.

STORY · Citric acid cycle

0–6 seconds: Under “Citric acid cycle,” an oval mitochondrial cutaway reveals the outer membrane, folded inner membrane and matrix. Calm English narration introduces the reaction diagram.

6–13 seconds: Labeled acetyl-CoA enters the diagram, not across the membrane; its two-carbon acetyl group joins four-carbon oxaloacetate, forming six-carbon citrate and releasing coenzyme A.

13–18 seconds: Citrate rearranges into labeled isocitrate without carbon loss.

18–25 seconds: Isocitrate becomes five-carbon α-ketoglutarate; labels mark the first CO₂ and NADH.

25–32 seconds: α-Ketoglutarate becomes four-carbon succinyl-CoA, releasing the second CO₂ and producing a second NADH. Neither departing CO₂ is tracked from the incoming acetyl pair.

32–38 seconds: Succinyl-CoA becomes succinate; ATP appears with the small qualification “GTP in some tissues.”

38–44 seconds: Beside the inner membrane, succinate becomes fumarate; enzyme-bound FAD becomes FADH₂ and stays bound.

44–52 seconds: Water is added to fumarate, forming malate. Malate is oxidized, regenerating oxaloacetate and producing the third NADH to close the loop.

52–60 seconds: Hold the loop with totals per acetyl-CoA: 2 CO₂, 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 ATP equivalent. Narration explains that reduced carriers supply electrons for subsequent ATP production.
