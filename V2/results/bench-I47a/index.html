RESEARCH · 8 facts
  1. (Day 0 after sowing; calendar date and publication date not stated.) The documented episode begins with red kidney beans planted in perlite. The authors explain that perlite supports the seed, permits aeration and makes the roots easy to view. This gives the film a specific observed sequence rather than an invented historical incident. ([passel2.unl.edu](https://passel2.unl.edu/view/lesson/ecfd27c27b15/5)) source
  2. (Days 1–2 after sowing; calendar dates not stated.) During days 1–2, the planted bean absorbs water and its seed coat swells. The coat may begin slipping off, although complete shedding can take days or may not occur. This directly supports the opening voice-over sentence. ([passel2.unl.edu](https://passel2.unl.edu/view/lesson/ecfd27c27b15/6)) source
  3. (Days 3–4 after sowing; calendar dates not stated.) The photographic sequence documents the embryonic root, or radicle, emerging from the bean seed on days 3–4. Show root emergence before the above-ground leafy shoot. ([passel2.unl.edu](https://passel2.unl.edu/view/lesson/ecfd27c27b15/7)) source
  4. (Days 4–6 after sowing; calendar dates not stated.) The growth timeline records continued radicle growth, root formation and hypocotyl growth during days 4–6. The transformation therefore takes days and must be compressed for an eight-second explainer. ([passel2.unl.edu](https://passel2.unl.edu/revision/lesson/ecfd27c27b15/66405/3)) source
  5. (Days 7–8 after sowing; calendar dates not stated.) By days 7–8, lateral roots are growing from the developing taproot, cotyledons are starting to appear and the first unifoliate leaves are peeking out. This provides a documented transition from a sprouted seed to a recognizable seedling. ([passel2.unl.edu](https://passel2.unl.edu/view/lesson/ecfd27c27b15/9)) source
  6. (Days 11–12 after sowing; calendar dates not stated.) Around days 11–12, the two unifoliate leaves unfold and expand, exposing the terminal apex. This is the outcome of the selected early-growth episode and a suitable final image. ([passel2.unl.edu](https://passel2.unl.edu/view/lesson/ecfd27c27b15/12)) source
  7. (Undated teaching document; applies during seedling growth.) General biological context, not a separate observation in the bean sequence: roots grow in the direction of gravity, called positive gravitropism; shoots grow away from gravity, called negative gravitropism. Downward root growth should not be presented as simply following a visible beam of light. ([fastplants.org](https://fastplants.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/WFPphysiology-06web.pdf)) source
  8. (Undated teaching document; applies during shoot growth.) General biological context: growing shoots can bend toward light through phototropism. The Wisconsin Fast Plants teaching guide distinguishes this response from gravitropism. For the exact narration, overhead illumination can communicate the shoot reaching upward toward light without implying that light alone determines its initial upward direction. ([fastplants.org](https://fastplants.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/WFPphysiology-06web.pdf)) source

STORY · Red Kidney Bean: Seed to Seedling

For the opening seven seconds, live-action macro photography shows a red kidney bean planted in white, angular, pearl-like perlite; its hard seed coat and visible hilum establish the seed’s appearance. [1]

The next eight seconds compress germination into simple animation. [2–5] The exact voice-over accompanies this sequence: “A seed takes in water. Roots grow downward, and a shoot reaches up toward the light.” [2, 7, 8] On days 1–2, the seed absorbs water and its coat swells rather than instantly disappearing. [2] On days 3–4, the radicle emerges before the leafy shoot. [3] During days 4–6, the radicle lengthens, roots form, and the hypocotyl grows. [4] The root extends downward while the shoot grows upward beneath overhead illumination. [7, 8] By days 7–8, smaller lateral roots branch from the main root, cotyledons appear, and the first unifoliate leaves peek out. [5]

The final fifteen seconds return to live-action time-lapse of days 11–12: two unifoliate leaves unfold and expand, exposing the terminal apex between them. [6] This paired-leaf seedling is the final image. [6]
