RESEARCH · 8 facts
  1. (December 15, 2006; historical account published December 15, 2022.) The aircraft was AA-1, the first prototype F-35A Lightning II, and its December 15, 2006 sortie was the F-35’s first flight. The Joint Program Office identifies the aircraft and episode in its historical account. ([jsf.mil](https://www.jsf.mil/NewsArticle/276?utm_source=openai)) source
  2. (December 15, 2006.) Lockheed Martin chief pilot Jon Beesley flew the inaugural sortie. He reported afterward that the aircraft had performed well. ([investors.lockheedmartin.com](https://investors.lockheedmartin.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lockheed-martin-f-35-lightning-ii-stealth-fighter-completes/?utm_source=openai)) source
  3. (December 15, 2006; Air Force report published December 16, 2006.) Takeoff occurred at 12:44 p.m. Central Standard Time at Lockheed Martin in Fort Worth, Texas. An official photograph records the aircraft lifting away from the runway with its landing gear extended. ([af.mil](https://www.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/128701/engineers-contribute-to-f-35-initial-flight-success/?utm_source=openai)) source
  4. (December 15, 2006 episode; variant description published December 15, 2022.) The F-35A is the conventional takeoff and landing variant, intended to operate from traditional airstrips. 'Conventional takeoff' is therefore an accurate on-screen label for this runway departure. ([jsf.mil](https://www.jsf.mil/NewsArticle/276?utm_source=openai)) source
  5. (First flight: December 15, 2006; Air Force specification marked current as of April 2014.) The F-35A has one engine: the Air Force lists one Pratt & Whitney F135-PW-100 turbofan. The contemporary first-flight release identifies the inaugural aircraft’s engine as a Pratt & Whitney F135. 'One engine' is accurate. ([af.mil](https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/478441/f-35a-lightning-ii-conventional-takeoff-and-landing-variant/sf2589626/f-35a-lightning-ii/?utm_source=openai)) source
  6. (December 15, 2006; report published December 16, 2006.) After departure, the aircraft climbed to 15,000 feet. The pilot performed maneuvers to evaluate handling and the operation of the engine and aircraft subsystems: this was a development test flight, not a combat mission. ([af.mil](https://www.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/128701/engineers-contribute-to-f-35-initial-flight-success/?utm_source=openai)) source
  7. (December 15, 2006.) The aircraft returned to land at 1:19 p.m. Central Standard Time, 35 minutes after departure. The eight-second film should depict an excerpt of takeoff and flight, not imply that the entire sortie lasted eight seconds. ([af.mil](https://www.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/128701/engineers-contribute-to-f-35-initial-flight-success/?utm_source=openai)) source
  8. (December 15, 2006 announcement.) The successful inaugural flight began the F-35 flight-test campaign. At the time, Lockheed Martin described a planned 12,000-hour program intended to validate extensive laboratory testing; that figure was a program plan, not hours already flown. ([investors.lockheedmartin.com](https://investors.lockheedmartin.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lockheed-martin-f-35-lightning-ii-stealth-fighter-completes/?utm_source=openai)) source
