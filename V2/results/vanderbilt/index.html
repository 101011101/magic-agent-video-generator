  1. (March 8–9, 1862) The immediate problem was CSS Virginia: its March 8 attack on Union ships at Hampton Roads, followed by its March 9 fight with USS Monitor, created the crisis behind Vanderbilt's offer. ([history.navy.mil](https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/browse-by-topic/wars-conflicts-and-operations/civil-war/cw-operations-and-engagements/1862-civil-war/hampton-roads.html?utm_source=openai)) source
  2. (March 17, 1862) Secretary of War Edwin Stanton escorted Cornelius Vanderbilt to meet President Abraham Lincoln at the White House in Washington, D.C. ([news.vanderbilt.edu](https://news.vanderbilt.edu/2011/04/11/the-commodores-civil-war/)) source
  3. (March 17, 1862) Vanderbilt believed his large steamship Vanderbilt could destroy Virginia by collision. Lincoln asked how much he would charge for the vessel's services. ([news.vanderbilt.edu](https://news.vanderbilt.edu/2011/04/11/the-commodores-civil-war/)) source
  4. (March 17, 1862) Instead of naming a price, Vanderbilt offered to donate the ship, conditional on controlling its preparations for battle. This is the episode's factual dramatic reversal; reconstructed dialogue should not be passed off as a verbatim transcript. ([news.vanderbilt.edu](https://news.vanderbilt.edu/2011/04/11/the-commodores-civil-war/)) source
  5. (Early 1862; precise proposal date not specified) Vanderbilt proposed filling the bow with concrete and reinforcing it with iron plating for use against Virginia. The Navy's history explicitly states that this particular modification was not performed. ([history.navy.mil](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/v/vanderbilt.html?utm_source=openai)) source
  6. (March 24 and summer 1862) The ship was transferred to the Navy on March 24. During summer 1862, the New York Navy Yard fitted her with a battery of fifteen guns. ([history.navy.mil](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/v/vanderbilt.html?utm_source=openai)) source
  7. (July 17, 1862) In a formal message to Congress, Lincoln confirmed that Vanderbilt had given the ocean steamer to the United States without payment in March, and recommended an appropriate acknowledgment. This is primary-source confirmation of the gift. ([govinfo.gov](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/SERIALSET-01123_00_00-007-0071-0000/pdf/SERIALSET-01123_00_00-007-0071-0000.pdf?utm_source=openai)) source
  8. (1864) Congress subsequently honored Vanderbilt with a gold medal in appreciation of his gift of the steamship to the Union Navy—a documented closing payoff for the film. ([collections.library.vanderbilt.edu](https://collections.library.vanderbilt.edu/repositories/2/archival_objects/656328?utm_source=openai)) source

STORY · Instead of a Price

An opening caption establishes the crisis: on March 8, 1862, CSS Virginia attacked Union ships at Hampton Roads; on March 9, it fought USS Monitor. These events prompted Cornelius Vanderbilt’s offer.

March 17, 1862. At the White House in Washington, D.C., Secretary of War Edwin Stanton escorts Cornelius Vanderbilt to meet President Abraham Lincoln.

A cutaway shows the large steamship Vanderbilt. Narration explains Vanderbilt’s belief that his vessel could destroy CSS Virginia by collision. Back at the meeting, narration conveys Lincoln’s question about how much Vanderbilt would charge for the vessel’s services.

The camera holds on Cornelius Vanderbilt as narration reveals his answer: instead of naming a price, he offers to donate the ship to the United States, provided he controls its preparations for battle.
