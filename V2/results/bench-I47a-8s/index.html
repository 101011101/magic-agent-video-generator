  1. (Day 0 after planting; calendar date not stated.) The documented subject is a red kidney bean growth sequence. At day 0, dry beans were planted in perlite, chosen to support seeds while allowing aeration and easy viewing of roots. ([passel2.unl.edu](https://passel2.unl.edu/revision/lesson/ecfd27c27b15/66405/5)) source
  2. (Days 1–2 after planting; calendar dates not stated.) During days 1–2, the planted bean absorbs water; its seed coat swells and may begin slipping off. This directly supports the opening voice-over sentence. ([passel2.unl.edu](https://passel2.unl.edu/revision/lesson/ecfd27c27b15/66405/6)) source
  3. (Day 3 in the timeline; days 3–4 on the photographic page; calendar dates not stated.) The sequence’s growth timeline places emergence of the radicle—the embryonic root—on day 3. Its accompanying photographic page documents emergence during days 3–4. ([passel2.unl.edu](https://passel2.unl.edu/revision/lesson/ecfd27c27b15/66405/3)) source
  4. (Days 5–6 after planting; calendar dates not stated.) By days 5–6, lateral roots begin forming off the radicle while the cotyledons continue swelling. The first leaves are becoming more defined but remain inside the seed coat. ([passel2.unl.edu](https://passel2.unl.edu/revision/lesson/ecfd27c27b15/66405/8)) source
  5. (Days 7–8 after planting; calendar dates not stated.) By days 7–8, the main root resembles a taproot, its lateral roots continue growing, and cotyledons and first leaves begin appearing. ([passel2.unl.edu](https://passel2.unl.edu/revision/lesson/ecfd27c27b15/66405/9)) source
  6. (Days 9–10 after planting; calendar dates not stated.) During days 9–10, the first leaves push past the cotyledons and rapid cell expansion makes the stems appear upright. The source also records different germination rates among the nine seeds pictured, so these timings should not be presented as universal. ([passel2.unl.edu](https://passel2.unl.edu/revision/lesson/ecfd27c27b15/66405/11)) source
  7. (Days 11–12 after planting; calendar dates not stated.) Around days 11–12, the two first unifoliate leaves unfold and expand, exposing the terminal growing tip: a clear endpoint for the animation’s transformation from seed to young seedling. ([passel2.unl.edu](https://passel2.unl.edu/revision/lesson/ecfd27c27b15/66405/12)) source
  8. (During seedling development; RHS page publication date not stated.) Downward root growth is a response to gravity, called positive geotropism. Seedling roots and shoots can orient themselves in response to gravity after germination; do not imply that roots grow downward because they are fleeing light. ([rhs.org.uk](https://www.rhs.org.uk/advice/understanding-plants/how-plants-sense-the-environment)) source
  9. (During seedling development; RHS page publication date not stated.) Shoots respond to both gravity and light: stems grow upward against gravity, and seedlings can bend toward a light source. Overhead lighting supports the brief’s simple image of a shoot reaching upward toward light without suggesting that underground upward orientation depends on light alone. ([rhs.org.uk](https://www.rhs.org.uk/advice/understanding-plants/how-plants-sense-the-environment)) source

STORY · Red Kidney Bean: Seed to Seedling

In the opening two seconds, a fixed widescreen live-action close-up shows a dry red kidney bean planted in perlite, its hard coat and hilum visible. Simple animation compresses the documented growth sequence: the bean absorbs water, swells, and its coat progressively loosens rather than disappearing. The voice-over begins: “A seed takes in water.”

From seconds two to five, the radicle emerges and extends downward in response to gravity; lateral roots begin forming as the cotyledons swell. The first leaves remain within the loosening coat before beginning to appear alongside the cotyledons. The narration continues: “Roots grow downward, and a shoot reaches up toward the light.”

In the final three seconds, the first leaves push upward past the cotyledons on an upright stem beneath overhead illumination. The two unifoliate leaves unfold and expand, revealing the terminal growing tip, with the developing root system visible below. The sequence ends on this young seedling, without on-screen text or music.
