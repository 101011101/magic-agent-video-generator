STORY · Citric acid cycle

Under “Citric acid cycle,” an oval mitochondrial cutaway shows a symbolic, arrow-linked loop in the matrix. Labels identify molecules as calm English narration follows the reactions. Acetyl-CoA supplies a two-carbon acetyl group to four-carbon oxaloacetate, forming six-carbon citrate; CoA-SH is released.

Citrate rearranges into six-carbon isocitrate. Isocitrate becomes five-carbon α-ketoglutarate: CO₂ leaves and the first NADH appears. Next, four-carbon succinyl-CoA forms, releasing a second CO₂ and producing a second NADH. The departing carbons are not the newly arrived acetyl carbons.

Succinyl-CoA becomes succinate, releasing CoA-SH; a GTP → ATP callout represents one ATP equivalent, not two additional products. Membrane-embedded succinate dehydrogenase converts succinate to fumarate as FAD becomes FADH₂. Water enters as fumarate becomes malate.

Malate becomes oxaloacetate, producing the third NADH. The regenerated oxaloacetate can accept another acetyl group, closing the loop. The completed turn shows two CO₂, three NADH, one FADH₂ and one ATP equivalent.
