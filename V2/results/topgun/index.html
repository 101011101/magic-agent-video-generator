Air Force formation-training guidance directs number two to acknowledge a visual signal with a head nod and to use radio to resolve confusion. This supports a restrained acknowledgement beat, but is not evidence of an F-35-specific attack gesture or exact naval combat dialogue. source

Six external infrared cameras provide imagery that can let the pilot see a view otherwise blocked by aircraft structure; the aircraft itself does not become transparent. source

Laser-guided bombs detect and track a target illuminated by a laser; they should not be portrayed as independently radar-seeking anti-ship missiles. source

STORY · Together, On Target

Over Meridian Range, Vega’s F-35C and Rook’s F-35C race above blue ocean, gray wings spread, twin tails canted outward. Guitar and drums punch through engine thunder.

Inside Vega’s F-35C, Vega turns a dark helmet toward Rook’s F-35C. Sunlight flashes across the broad reflective visor. A ribbed oxygen hose crosses an olive-green flight suit.

“Rook, you with me?” Vega’s radio crackles.

“Right here, Vega.”

Inside Rook’s F-35C, Rook looks across at Vega and gives one firm nod. Vega nods back.

Ahead, the gray frigate Harrow floats beneath a tall mast. A caption reads: “MERIDIAN RANGE. HARROW: DECOMMISSIONED TARGET.”

On Vega’s wide touchscreen, Harrow sits beneath a crosshair. “LASER” illuminates beside the image.

Vega’s F-35C and Rook’s F-35C bank toward Harrow. Each releases a GBU-12 Paveway II bomb from a wing pylon. The camera follows the two GBU-12 Paveway II bombs downward, narrow noses leading, rear fins spread.

Two impacts punch fire and spray along Harrow. Smoke rolls above the mast.

Vega’s F-35C and Rook’s F-35C climb together. The guitar cuts out.
