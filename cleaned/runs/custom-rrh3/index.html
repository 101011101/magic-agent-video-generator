Story (prose → step script)

Little Red Riding Hood skipped along the woodland path, carrying a basket of cake and a corked bottle for Grandmother. A charcoal-gray wolf stepped from the ferns. “Where are you going?” he asked sweetly. Red pointed toward the cottage. “Grandmother isn’t feeling well.” “Then take her some flowers,” suggested the wolf. While Red wandered among the blossoms, he hurried away. At the cottage, he imitated Red’s voice. Grandmother opened the door. Before she could escape, he swallowed her whole! Then he pulled on her white nightcap and climbed into bed. Red arrived and set her basket on the table. She approached the bed, clutching her cape. “Grandmother, what big ears you have!” “All the better to hear you with.” “What big eyes you have!” “All the better to see you with.” Red backed away, trembling. “What big teeth you have!” “All the better to eat you with!” The wolf leaped from bed and swallowed her. With his belly round, he lay down and snored. A passing huntsman heard the dreadful noise. Inside, he spotted the nightcap and understood. Taking sewing scissors from the shelf, he carefully snipped open the sleeping wolf’s belly. Red scrambled out, gasping, followed by Grandmother. They clung together, shaking with relief. Red fetched six heavy stones from outside. The huntsman placed them inside the wolf and stitched him closed with a threaded needle. The wolf woke and tried to flee, but the stones dragged him down. He fell dead beside the bed. Red hugged Grandmother tightly. Together they shared the cake with their rescuer, and Red promised never to stray from the path again.
