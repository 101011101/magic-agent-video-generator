# Video Script Brief: "The Shell" — Lighthouse Keeper Short Film

## Goal and core idea
Write the script for a 2-minute wordless short film. A lone lighthouse keeper finds a glowing shell, brings it home, and when its strange power puts out the lighthouse lamp, she uses that same power in a storm to relight the lamp and save the ships. At dawn she returns the shell to the sea. The idea it must get across: something wondrous and borrowed — gratefully used, then let go. Themes of solitude, duty, quiet heroism, and the melancholy of returning what was never yours to keep.

## Audience and platform
- **Audience:** General adult audience that appreciates art-house / animated shorts and atmospheric storytelling.
- **Platform:** Not specified. Assume online viewing (YouTube, Vimeo, film-festival-style screening), full-screen on desktop or TV.

## Format, duration and aspect ratio
- **Format:** Narrative short film, multiple scenes and shots, no dialogue, music score only.
- **Duration:** 2 minutes (120 seconds), stated by user.
- **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 widescreen (assumed; cinematic. 2.39:1 letterbox inside 16:9 is an acceptable stylistic choice).

## Tone and style
- **Tone:** Melancholy, quiet, contemplative, tender; tension during the storm; bittersweet resolution.
- **Visual style:** Painterly — looks like a moving oil or gouache painting: visible brushstroke texture, soft edges, rich atmospheric colour. Consistent style across every shot.
- **Colour arc (suggested):** cool pale pinks/blues at dawn → warm amber interior → deep blue-black night with the shell's glow → violent grey-green storm with the lamp's gold beam → soft, washed gold morning.
- **Pacing:** Slow and lingering in the opening and ending; faster cutting in the storm climax.
- **Sound:** Score only, plus optional natural sound (waves, wind, rain, the shell's hum). No dialogue, no voiceover, no on-screen text except optional title/credits.
- The shell's glow and hum are the film's key visual/aural motif and must be consistent in colour and character every time they appear.

## Required story beats (in order, with suggested timing)
1. **Dawn, the beach (0:00–0:20):** Establish the lighthouse, the sea, the keeper alone. She walks the shore and finds the glowing shell.
2. **Bringing it home (0:20–0:35):** She carries it to the lighthouse cottage and places it somewhere meaningful; quiet domestic moments.
3. **Night, the hum (0:35–0:55):** The shell hums and pulses. The lighthouse lamp flickers and goes out. Darkness over the sea.
4. **The storm and the climb (0:55–1:25):** A storm rises; ships' lights are visible at sea in danger. She takes the shell and climbs the tower stairs through wind and rain.
5. **Relighting (1:25–1:40):** At the top, she uses the shell to relight the lamp. The beam returns, sweeping the sea.
6. **Ships saved (1:40–1:50):** The ships turn away from the rocks / reach safety.
7. **Morning, letting go (1:50–2:00):** Calm morning. She returns the shell to the beach where she found it, and watches the tide; closing image.

## Assumptions (not stated by the user)
- **The keeper:** A solitary woman, middle-aged to older, weathered, practical clothing (wool sweater, oilskin coat in the storm). Exact look is to be defined in the script and kept consistent.
- **Setting:** A rocky, remote coast with a classic white (or white-and-red) stone lighthouse and an attached keeper's cottage; era is timeless/early 20th century, with an oil or early-electric lamp.
- **The shell:** A spiral seashell, palm-sized, emitting a soft cool glow (e.g. pale blue-green or opal). How it "relights" the lamp is left poetic and visual (e.g. its light flows into the lamp), not explained.
- **Cause of the lamp failing:** Implied, not explained — the shell's hum seems to draw the light away, creating the link that she must later reverse.
- **Ships:** One or two sailing or fishing vessels at sea during the storm.
- **Animation/style:** Painterly animated or painterly-rendered look, not photoreal live action.
- Opening and closing shots mirror each other (beach at dawn / beach in morning) to frame the story.
- Optional title card at the start or end; no credits text unless provided.
